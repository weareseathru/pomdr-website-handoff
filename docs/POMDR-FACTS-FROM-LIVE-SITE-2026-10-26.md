# POMDR facts, as the live site states them

Every factual claim on www.pomdr.org (served as peaceofminddogrescue.org),
pulled from the full capture of 2026-10-22 (`docs/LIVE-SITE-WORDS-2026-10-22.md`)
and organized by subject. This is the ruler for the fine-tooth-comb pass on the
new site: a sentence on the new site that is not supported here is either a
fact we must confirm with Carie, or a sentence to change.

Wording in quotation marks is the live site's own. Where the live site
disagrees with itself, both versions are shown and the item is listed again in
section 15 for Carie to settle. Nothing here is from the new site.

---

## 1. Identity

- Legal name: **Peace of Mind Dog Rescue**. Short form POMDR.
- "a 501(c)(3) nonprofit organization founded in October 2009."
- Tax ID (TIN): **27-1154816**.
- Mission, verbatim: "Our mission is to be a resource and advocate for senior
  dogs and senior people on California's Central Coast. We focus on helping
  dogs and people from Monterey, Santa Cruz, and San Benito counties."
- The three programs, in the site's own words (home and About):
  - **Intake & Adoptions**: "We find loving homes for dogs whose guardians can
    no longer care for them and for senior dogs in shelters."
  - **Helping Paw**: "We provide volunteer and financial assistance so seniors
    can keep their pets with them as long as possible."
  - **Perpetual Care**: "We make pre-arrangements to take in dogs should their
    guardians become unable to care for them."
- Lifetime commitment, verbatim: "We have a lifetime commitment to all the dogs
  who come into our care. They will either be adopted into a wonderful,
  permanent home, or will live out their lives in one of our foster homes."
- Vision, verbatim: "Our vision is to model lifetime care for dogs and all
  companion animals to help bring about a positive change in the way society
  thinks about and treats senior dogs, and to create better lives for them
  through rescue, foster, adoption, hospice and education."
- Meta description used site-wide: "Peace of Mind Dog Rescue is dedicated to
  finding new loving homes for dogs whose guardians can no longer care for them
  due to illness, death, or other challenging circumstances, and to finding
  homes for senior dogs in animal shelters."
- Jobs page summary: "We take in senior dogs from shelter as well as dogs from
  senior people no longer able to care for them. We also provide veterinary
  care to low-income pet guardians."

## 2. Which dogs POMDR takes, and how many

- "Although we specialize in senior dogs, we also get younger dogs surrendered
  to us from senior guardians." (Adopt page.) The adoptable list on 2026-10-22
  included dogs aged 2 months to 15 years. **Not all POMDR dogs are seniors.**
- Two intake streams: senior dogs from shelters, and dogs of any age from
  senior guardians who can no longer care for them. Plus Perpetual Care
  pre-arranged intakes.
- Shelters that bring dogs to POMDR's attention (Thank You page): Hollister
  Animal Shelter, Hitchcock Road Animal Services, Pacific Grove Animal Control,
  Santa Cruz Animal Shelter, San Jose Animal Services, SPCA for Monterey County.
- Intake criteria (Placing Your Dog): "we will need to meet the dog. Dogs who
  come to POMDR must be good with other dogs (no aggression) and must be good
  with people (no bite history or propensity to bite)." Medical cases that
  "might be difficult for us to take on, e.g. Epilepsy, Diabetes, Paralysis,
  Megaesophagus, Advanced Dementia. Medical intakes are evaluated on a case by
  case basis." Dogs that are not candidates can still submit the questionnaire
  for referrals.
- Dogs in care at one time: "We typically have about 80 dogs in our care at any
  given time" (Foster Needs) **versus** "We have an average of 60 senior dogs
  in our care at any given time" (Wish List). Both live. See section 15.
- "We also typically have up to 15 dogs waiting to get into foster homes."
- Where dogs live: "We do not have a facility where our dogs are kept. All of
  our dogs live in foster homes as members of the family" (Fostering). **But**
  the clinic has doggie suites that "house the dogs who come into our program
  while they are getting their veterinary care completed," and several
  foster-needs write-ups say a dog "is currently cozied up in a doggy suite,
  awaiting her foster family." Accurate phrasing: dogs live in foster homes;
  some wait in a suite at the clinic until a foster home opens.
- Courtesy listings: "The following dogs are not under the care of Peace of
  Mind Dog Rescue. We provide these listings as a courtesy to the community."
  Contact is the person named in the write-up, not POMDR.
- Hospice care: "Sometimes when we bring a senior dog into our care, we find
  out they have a life threatening medical condition. For some of our dogs who
  are expected to live only a few months, we provide Hospice Care." Hospice
  dogs can be sponsored; "POMDR will cover all medical expenses for dogs in
  hospice care." (Note the About page's sentence: sometimes a dog "should not
  have to endure one more move or life change" and stays in a foster home for
  life.)
- Dog listings show "Looks like:" for breed, an age (often "(estimated)"), a
  weight, and status flags: Foster needed, Adoption pending, Recently Adopted.
- Status counts on the live site are not stated; the Adopted page is a long
  archive of past adoptions.

## 3. Adoption

- Vet care on intake: "Each dog in our program receives a thorough veterinary
  exam upon intake. The senior dogs also get a senior blood panel and in many
  cases a dental cleaning and any necessary extractions. Each dog is altered,
  microchipped, and vaccinated unless our veterinarian advises against
  sterilization surgery or vaccines due to age or other health concerns."
  The clinic page adds heartworm test and urinalysis.
- **Adoption fees: $155 to $305**, "depending upon the age and health of the
  dog and expected future medical expenses that will be taken on by the
  adopter." No senior-adopter discount is stated anywhere.
- Process: "The first step to meeting an adoptable dog is to fill out an online
  adoption questionnaire. If you are a good match, we will connect you with the
  dog's foster to talk more, then set up a meet and greet with the dog."
- "Responses to our adoption questionnaires are typically sent via email -
  please check your spam/junk file."
- Conditions: "All adopters must agree to an initial home check and annual
  follow up calls throughout the life of the dog." Placing Your Dog adds
  "Screening of potential adopters including home visit and reference check"
  and "Annual follow-ups and home visits."
- Meeting dogs: by appointment through the questionnaire and the foster; the
  footer says "Adoptable dogs available by appointment only." Dogs also appear
  at adoption events.
- Adoption events: "We hold adoption events most Saturdays and some Sundays
  around the community."
- "Our website is updated several times a week."
- Other rescues the site points to: Animal Friends Rescue Project, Hitchcock
  Road Animal Services, Santa Cruz SPCA, Santa Cruz County Animal Shelter, SPCA
  of Monterey County, Senior Dogs Project, Muttville, Old Dog Haven, NORCAL
  Golden Retriever Rescue.
- Why a senior dog (the site's six reasons, as headings): Every Dog Deserves a
  Second Chance; They Are Calmer, Quieter and Less Destructive; What You See Is
  What You Get; They Usually Have Basic Training; They Are NOT "Problem Dogs";
  Every Day is Special. Key sentence: "The vast majority of our senior dogs
  come to POMDR because their guardians have passed away, need to move into
  assisted living or simply because they can no longer care for their dog."

## 4. Helping Paw

- Who: "assists seniors, hospice patients and other pet guardians facing
  challenging times in keeping their beloved dogs **and cats**." Helping Paw
  covers cats as well as dogs.
- Two halves: financial assistance and volunteer assistance. Plus supplies.
- **Financial assistance**
  - Geography: Monterey, Santa Cruz and San Benito Counties.
  - Income: "You must be able to demonstrate financial need."
  - Covers: "Urgent medical needs that will alleviate an animal's suffering."
  - Does not cover: routine veterinary care. Flea treatment by pre-arrangement,
    picked up at the Pacific Grove office.
  - **Maximum assistance: $750. "We can help with up to $2,750 for major
    medical emergencies."**
  - Exam and estimate: best if the pet has had an exam and there is an
    estimate; "In some circumstances, we can cover the cost of the exam."
  - "a limit of assistance once per year."
  - Spay/neuter required; POMDR covers it at the time of treatment if needed.
  - Spanish application exists; Spanish note on the page says calling
    (831) 718-9122 is possible but "will delay the processing of your request."
- **Volunteer assistance**
  - Provides: "Short term dog walking for emergency situations, emergency short
    term foster care or boarding for people who are in the hospital or
    recovering from illness or surgery, transportation to vet or groomer for
    disabled and senior pet guardians."
  - Requirement: "all dogs must pass a behavioral evaluation prior to receiving
    dog walking or foster care services. This evaluation typically takes place
    at our office in Pacific Grove."
  - Geography: the three counties, "Subject to volunteer availability in the
    area."
  - "There are no income restrictions for Volunteer Assistance. Donations are
    welcomed."
- **Supplies** (donated, as available, **picked up at headquarters in Pacific
  Grove**): dog and cat food, flea prevention, collars, leashes, crates, ID
  tags, blankets, dog jackets, sweaters, raincoats. POMDR does not state that
  it delivers.
- Phone fallback, verbatim: "If you are unable to fill out an application or
  need more information, you can call us at (831) 718-9122. Please be aware
  that this will delay the processing of your request."
- **Max's Helping Paws Fund** (a POMDR fund since MHP's programs moved to
  POMDR): origin is Maximillian Amadeus, d. 2016; founders Dyana Klein and
  Dr. Jonathan Fradkin; MHP "helped over 2,400 pets" 2016 to 2025; programs
  "Get 'Em to the Vet, Short-Term, and Continued Care"; purpose "financial
  assistance to low income pet guardians needing unexpected, urgent or
  emergent veterinary care including surgery and specialist visits." The page
  says "the financial cap for veterinary assistance has increased fivefold,
  from $500 to up to $2,500 per case." (The Helping Paw page says $750 and
  $2,750. See section 15.)
- Funding named on the site: Animal Welfare and Assistance Group, $204,300 for
  Helping Paw; Grey Muzzle Foundation $15,000; Giant Steps Foundation $7,500.
  Partners: Max's Helping Paws Foundation and BirchBark Foundation.
- Origin story of the Bauer Center involves Helping Paw: volunteers "walked
  Mattie and Morgan every day for three months as well as fed them, brushed
  them, and took them to vet and grooming appointments."

## 5. Placing your dog (Guardian Surrender Program)

- Who: "senior dogs from shelters and ... dogs from senior guardians who can no
  longer care for them," in the three counties. Any-age dogs from senior
  guardians.
- How: the online intake questionnaire. Phone help available but delays
  processing (English and Spanish notes).
- Criteria and medical limits: section 2.
- If accepted, POMDR provides, verbatim list: placement in a foster home until
  adopted; high quality dog food; coverage of medical needs while in care;
  socialization and companionship; "A safety net for the lifetime of your dog
  should she ever need to find a new home at any point in her life"; grooming
  and flea control; screening of adopters including home visit and reference
  check; annual follow-ups and home visits.
- "Any donation made will also help us rescue more dogs left behind." No
  surrender fee is stated.

## 6. Perpetual Care Program (Lifetime Care)

- Purpose: for guardians who plan ahead and have no friend or family member to
  take the dog. "guaranteed a loving, warm, and safe home for their dog(s)."
- How it works: "we suggest that you set up a legal pet trust for the lifetime
  care expenses for your dog ... We also suggest that you make an annual gift
  in any amount to POMDR." Those unable to do either should "contact us to
  discuss the next steps."
- Eligibility: "We will need to meet your dog ... will depend upon the dog's
  age, size, temperament, and any special medical needs." The FAQ adds: "POMDR
  will accept your dog regardless of his or her physical condition" once
  enrolled.
- Enrollment steps (FAQ): talk with a board member; fill out a Pet Profile
  form; set up the pet trust with an attorney.
- **Cost: "No. ... There is no membership fee or cost to you during your
  lifetime,"** a donation is encouraged.
- What happens: "POMDR will become your dog's legal guardian ... as soon as we
  receive notice of your death or incapacitation." Dog goes to a qualified
  foster home; if none is available, "a comfortable boarding facility with
  regular walks and outings provided by our volunteers."
- Outcome: "we cannot absolutely guarantee that your dog will be adopted, the
  great majority of our dogs find new homes." Lifetime commitment either way.
- Trust remainder: "At the time of your dog's death, any remainder of the trust
  would go into POMDR's general fund."
- Free pet trust: Clark Ruggiero Law Office "Will create a free pet trust for
  people leaving a dog to Peace of Mind Dog Rescue" (Resources).
- Press: an NPR (KAZU) story about the program.

## 7. Fostering and foster needs

- "Foster volunteers are the heart and soul of what we do."
- To foster: fill out the volunteer application (same form as all volunteering).
- What POMDR provides fosters (Foster Needs): "supplies such as crate, bed,
  collar, harness, leash, id tag, bowls, toys, flea prevention. We also cover
  all medical expenses. We ask that our foster homes provide the food and a
  loving, safe home."
- The Volunteer page is softer on food: "Sometimes we may have donated food to
  provide our foster homes with, but, at times, you may also be required to
  provide high quality food for your foster dog at your expense." And "Peace
  of Mind covers any approved medical expenses."
- Support: "a foster manual, training, an emergency contact number, and
  ongoing support and behavior troubleshooting from a foster mentor assigned
  to work directly with you."
- Foster-to-adopt is allowed: "You may also want to foster as a way of finding
  the perfect match for your home."
- Popular dogs may already be taken; "some dogs may have medical or behavioral
  needs, which will be disclosed (if known) when you inquire."
- Dogs needing a foster: some are already in a suite at the clinic; some are
  not yet in the program and "need a foster before entering the POMDR program."
- Foster write-ups end with three routes: existing volunteers email; new
  volunteers apply; adopters fill out the adoption questionnaire.

## 16. Things the live site does not say (so the new site should not either, unless Carie confirms)

- A lifetime total of dogs adopted or people helped. (The new site's "4,500+
  senior dogs adopted" came from staff on 2026-08-01, not from the website.)
- That every POMDR dog is a senior, or that every dog is seen by appointment
  at the Bauer Center.
- That POMDR delivers food or supplies; the site says pick-up in Pacific Grove.
- A senior-adopter fee discount or waiver.
- That Helping Paw is for seniors only; it is "seniors, hospice patients and
  other pet guardians facing challenging times," dogs and cats.
- That Perpetual Care "costs nothing" in absolute terms; the site says no fee
  or cost during your lifetime, with a pet trust funding the care after.
