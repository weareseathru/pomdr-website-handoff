# Leveling up the voice: what we add, what we change, what we remove, and why

**Proposal, written 2026-10-19 for Andrew Z.** Built from a full read of every word on the new site (`SITE-COPY-2026-10-19.md`, 41 pages, 17,100 words), the live site's archived voice, and two references Andrew asked me to study: the Center for Nonprofit Excellence's four principles of high-converting copy, and charity: water's site as the example of those principles done well. Nothing here is written yet. Every rewrite below is shown before and after so staff approve words, not ideas. Nothing invents a fact; where a number or a name is needed, it is marked as POMDR's to supply.

---

## 1. What the references teach, and what they do not

**The four principles (CNE)** are right and short: lead with the people and animals helped, not the process; put the mission above the fold; write at an eighth-grade level; make every button say what happens. Worth saying plainly: CNE's own homepage breaks three of its four rules. Its headline is "Celebrating 20 Years!", its buttons say "Learn More" five times, and it carries no impact number. The principles are the lesson, not the site.

**charity: water** is the site that keeps all four. What it does, in its own words:

- One promise as the headline: "Bring clean and safe water to every person on the planet." Not a tagline about the organization; a thing the reader can join.
- Impact stated as counts a reader can picture: "209,241 water projects funded", "21,641,908 people will be served", each with a one-line definition of what the number means.
- Money translated into people: "A gift of just $20 a month is enough to bring six people, an entire family, clean water each year." And "$40 can end a walk for water."
- Proof as a section, not a claim: "We prove every project", "We're an open book", "You have our word, and more."
- Every button is an outcome: "Sponsor Now", "Give Today", "Browse Fundraisers". Never "Learn more" as the primary action.

**What we must not copy.** charity: water is a global organization with a hundred million dollars behind it, and its voice is bright, fast and sure. POMDR is forty people in Pacific Grove taking the dogs nobody else will, talking to a widow about her terrier. Our register is quieter, slower and more tender, and that is the brand. The principles transfer. The pitch does not. "Bold" at POMDR means "We will not put your dog down for being old," which the intake page already says, and which is braver than anything on charity: water's homepage.

## 2. The POMDR voice, stated once

Five rules, drawn from the sentences on the site that already work. Everything in sections 3 to 5 serves these.

1. **The reader is in the sentence.** "Money should not be the reason you lose your dog." Not "Helping Paw provides financial assistance."
2. **A promise is said as a promise.** "We have a lifetime commitment to all the dogs who come into our care." Not "POMDR is dedicated to."
3. **Impact is a count of real dogs and real people, with what it cost.** "4,500 dogs" is a start. "Every dog gets a senior blood panel and, most of them, a dental" is the better sentence, because a reader can picture it.
4. **Honest about limits, in the same breath.** "Some extreme medical cases might be difficult for us to take on." Keep every one of these. They are what make the promises believable.
5. **Short words, short sentences, no process.** Eighth grade on the program pages, sixth on the care pages. No "processing of your request." No "subject to availability." If staff would not say it on the phone, it does not go on the page.

## 3. What we add

### 3.1 One mission sentence, everywhere the mission is stated

Today the mission is told six ways (homepage, Why, About, footer, Volunteer, Clinic). Proposed single sentence, in POMDR's words from the live site reordered for the reader:

Three options for Carie, so she chooses rather than approves. The review of this proposal pointed out that the first draft had four "we" words and no "you", and left out the third pillar of POMDR's founding: dogs whose person has died or can no longer care for them, who are not always senior dogs. All three below carry that pillar.

> **A.** We find homes for senior dogs and for dogs whose people can no longer care for them, and we help senior people keep the dogs they love. Every dog who comes to us is ours for life.
>
> **B.** A loving home for every senior dog, and for every dog whose person can no longer care for them. A helping hand so senior people keep the dogs they love. Every dog who comes to us is safe for life.
>
> **C.** Senior dogs, and dogs who have lost their people, find a home here. Senior people get help keeping theirs. And every dog who comes to us is ours for life.

A is the fullest. B reads at sixth grade and is the one I would put on the homepage. C is the shortest and the one for the footer and the share line. The reviewer also offered a "Together, we give..." version; I have left it out on purpose, because "Together, we" is charity: water's register, the one thing section 1 says not to borrow. Used verbatim on the homepage above the fold, the About band, the footer, and the share description. Needs: Carie's choice.

### 3.5 Concrete microcopy, every button an outcome

Buttons that say what happens. Current and proposed, from the pages:

| Today | Proposed | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Donate now | Fund a dog's care | Donate band |
| See all adoptable dogs | Meet all 83 waiting dogs *(count live)* | Homepage |
| Start a volunteer application | Join the volunteer team | Volunteer band |
| Open the form in its own page | Open the form full size | Every form page |
| Learn more / About Helping Paw (as a primary) | Get help keeping your dog | Get help card |
| Ways to give | Every way to give | Hospice |
| See the roles first | See the eleven ways to help | Volunteer application |
| Help pay for their care | Sponsor a hospice dog | Hospice band |

Rule: a button names the thing the reader gets, in four words or fewer where it can. Two refinements from the review, both right: "Pay for a dog's care" reads like an invoice, so it is "Fund a dog's care"; "Tell us what you can give" sounds like an ask, so it is "Join the volunteer team". Giving is an act of care, not a bill, and volunteering is joining, not supplying.

### 3.6 A dog-story standard, for the admin

Dog stories are the most-read words on the site and the only ones without a standard. Adele's story (quiet, specific, foster's own observation) is the model. Proposed prompt for the intake form, which goes to Ronnie as part of the harmony proposal:

> "Write two or three short paragraphs as if you were telling a neighbor about this dog over coffee. Start with one real moment from your day together: what they do when you pick up the leash, where they curl up when the sun comes in. Say what they love and what kind of home helps them feel safe. Skip breed, age and weight; the page already shows those. End with a warm truth, not a question."

The first draft said "open with who this dog is at home", and the review was right that most people would answer that with "a sweet, quiet boy who likes naps", which is nothing a reader can picture. One concrete moment is what Adele's story has and Ahi Tuna's does not. Three rules for the page stay: no "Meet [Name]" opener, no question ending, no puns longer than one line.

### 3.7 The named person

charity: water shows "The executive team." POMDR's care pages should say who answers, and the review caught the risk in the first draft: hard-coding "You will reach Shanel or Alejandra" makes two people a bottleneck, confuses the caller who gets someone else, and goes stale the month a role changes. The line that keeps the trust signal and protects the team:

> "When you call (831) 718-9122, Monday to Saturday, you will not get a call center or a machine. You will talk with Shanel, Alejandra, or another member of our small intake team here in Pacific Grove."

Names stay, with cover. Needs: POMDR-47 items 6 and 7, and a note in Design.md that this line is reviewed whenever the roster changes.

### 4.2 The carried-over paragraphs, rewritten in the care voice

These sit beside warm new copy and the seam shows. Before and after:

**Adoption process, step one.** Today: "Our dogs are kept in loving foster homes, so the first step to meeting an adoptable dog is the online questionnaire. Responses are typically sent by email; please check your spam or junk folder for the latest updates on your adoption." Proposed: "Every dog lives in a foster home, so the first step is the questionnaire. We reply by email within a few days. If you do not hear from us, check your spam folder, or call."

**Surrender, step one.** Today: "Fill out the online intake questionnaire. If you need help with it, call us at (831) 718-9122; please be aware that this will delay the processing of your request." Proposed: "Tell us about your dog in the questionnaire, or call (831) 718-9122 and we will fill it in with you."

**Perpetual Care, step one.** Today: "Contact POMDR and talk with a board member to determine whether your dog is appropriate for our program. Eligibility depends on the dog's age, size, temperament and any special medical needs, and we will need to meet your dog." Proposed: "Call us. A board member will talk with you about your dog, and we will come and meet them. Age, size, temperament and health all matter, and none of them rules a dog out on its own."

**Helping Paw, volunteer help.** Today: "Short term dog walking for emergency situations; emergency short term foster care or boarding for people who are in hospital or recovering from illness or surgery; transportation to the vet or groomer for disabled and senior pet guardians." Proposed: "A volunteer walks your dog when you cannot. If you go into the hospital, we foster or board your dog until you are back home. If you cannot drive, we take them to the vet or the groomer."

**Donate, monthly.** Today: "Become a sustaining donor and pledge a recurring monthly donation. Your credit card will be charged your pledged amount at the first of every month." Proposed: "Give the same amount on the first of every month. It is what lets us say yes to the next dog before the money is in. Change or stop it any time."

### 4.5 Repetition made deliberate

"You can stop part way through and come back to it" appears on seven form pages and "Any trouble at all, call" on five. Keep both; they are reassurance at the anxious moment. Remove the rest: the four mission variants (3.1), the four Helping Paw lists (4.3), the "No dog is ever left behind" paragraph that appears on Hospice and Why word for word (keep on Hospice; Why now has the promise strip).

## 5. What we remove, and why

- **Every "Learn more" and bare "Learn More" as a primary action.** There are few, which is good. Replace the ones that remain with the outcome (3.5).
- **Process language a visitor does not need:** "processing of your request", "subject to volunteer availability in the area" (say "where we have a volunteer near you"), "evaluated case by case" (say "we will talk about it"), "consistent with the information in their Pet Profile".
- **Question endings on dog stories** ("Could that be with you?") and "Meet [Name]" openers. Staff-written, so by standard (3.6), not by edit.
- **"Our team is awesome."** Replace with a sentence that shows it: "Twenty-seven people, most with a POMDR dog of their own."
- **The second "No dog is ever left behind" block** on Why, now that the promise strip carries the same promise.
- **"It is a promise, not a program"** on the homepage's Perpetual Care card. A nice line that undercuts the word "program" used in the page title two taps away.
- **Four small errors:** "ExclusiveYou must apply" on Resources, the stray bracket in "(I've Got a) Golden Ticket)", "Alberston" for Albertson, and the sponsor page's "You are sponsoring . Thank you." when no dog is named (hide the line until a name is present).

## 6. What does not change

- Every verbatim quote, including grammar and "fur baby." They are the proof.
- Every promise already made, and every honest limit stated.
- The care-page voice: "You are doing the right thing by asking." "We will not rush you." These are the standard the rest is being raised to.
- Phone first on every care page. charity: water never shows a phone number. POMDR always does, and should.
- No urgency, no guilt, no countdowns, no sad eyes behind bars. The national direct-response style raises quick money from strangers and costs the trust of the older guardian who is afraid of being judged. POMDR's "You are doing the right thing by asking" is the opposite stance, and it is the brand.
- The two colors, the band, the button above the text.
- Spanish on the Helping Paw and Surrender paths.

## 10. Peers, not aspirations: Muttville and Best Friends

charity: water is the model for method. Muttville (San Francisco, since 2007, "14,639 dogs rescued") and Best Friends Animal Society (Kanab, since 1984) are the comparisons that matter, because they do the same work. Read on 2026-10-19, quoted exactly.

**Muttville says the lifetime promise almost as we do.** "Muttville remains committed to the dogs for the remainder of their lives and will accept dogs back should it not work out." And: "our commitment to their well-being never ends." Ours is the same promise in fewer words, and it is said more often on our site than on theirs. Keep that.

**Muttville's "Seniors for Seniors" is the clearest program sentence either of us has.** "Senior dogs + senior humans = a perfect match." Then the terms: "if you are 62+ years of age, we waive our $250 adoption fee", and a welcome kit "that includes your dog's harness and collar, a leash, dog bowls", "a starter supply of food and medication, a doggy bed". That is CNE's "make microcopy concrete" principle applied to a whole program. POMDR's equivalent is Helping Paw, and nothing on our Helping Paw page is that concrete about what a person walks away with. Section 4.3's single list is the start; the welcome-kit style sentence is the finish. **Question for POMDR:** is there any fee relief for senior adopters, as Muttville's "Over 62? Your adoption is free!"? If yes, it is one of the most persuasive sentences the site could carry. If no, nothing is invented.

**Muttville's fostering line is ours too.** "Give a senior mutt a soft place to land." We use "a soft place to land" on the homepage and the Why page. It is a phrase of the field, not of either rescue, so there is no conflict, but we should not lean on it as if it were ours alone.

**Muttville's weakness is our opening.** Its homepage headline is "Welcome to the Wagcam!" and its About page has no mission or values section at all; the mission appears only as a tagline, "We're changing the way the world thinks about and treats older dogs." Its How to Adopt page opens with "We can't wait to find you your new best friend!" and its newsletter promises to "email you sparingly and very cutely." That voice is bright and a little arch, and it works for a city rescue with a live-streamed loft. POMDR's reader is older and often arrives in a hard moment. Our quieter register is a position, not a gap.

**Best Friends writes in the second person from the first word.** The homepage headline is "you're saving pets' lives." The subhead is "Happiness is a best friend." Every section opens with the reader: "Wherever you live, whatever your experience or interests, there are countless ways to volunteer." That is the pronoun lesson from section 9, done at scale. Our headlines in 4.1 move the same way.

**Best Friends proves itself with a spending split, in numbers.** "87%" to programs and services, "4%" management and general, "9%" fundraising, under a section called "Transparency, accountability, and trust". POMDR publishes its 990s but never says the split. One line under the promise block, from the latest return (2024, or the 2025 audited figures, so the proof is not three years old), framed so the rest of the dollar is not read as waste, since paying the people who answer the phone and keeping the clinic open is caring for the dogs: "From our latest Form 990: X cents of every dollar goes to veterinary care, foster support, adoption and Helping Paw grants for the dogs and people we serve." Use it if the program share is strong (about 82 percent or more); if it is not, say nothing rather than explain. **Needs: POMDR, from the latest 990.**

**Best Friends asks adopters for their story.** "Tell us about it, and share a photo or video", with a "Share My Story" button. That is the adoption-day questions from the timeline proposal (POMDR-44), as a public ask. Worth a line on the Happy tails page once the Hub can receive them.

**Best Friends states the stakes.** "every 80 seconds" a dog or cat is still killed in shelters. POMDR's equivalent stake is specific to senior dogs, and it is already in our Why band: "Older dogs are at the highest risk in shelters and the least likely to be chosen." It needs a source line the way charity: water gives its numbers a definition. **Needs: the citation from the research synthesis** (`RESEARCH-SENIORS-ADOPTION-AND-THE-WEB-2026-10-17.md` has it).

**All three peers connect lifetime care to a gift in a will.** POMDR's donate page already lists Planned Giving and Legacy Donor, but the Perpetual Care page, where an older owner is already thinking about the day they are gone, never mentions it. One gentle sentence there, after the enrollment steps: *"Many people who enroll a dog also remember POMDR in their will or trust, so that the promise we make to their dog can be made to the next one. Our planned giving page says how."* Nothing new is claimed; it links two pages that already exist.

**What neither peer has, and POMDR does.** Helping Paw and Perpetual Care. Muttville has no program for keeping a senior person's dog at home, and no enrollment for the day an owner cannot care for their dog. Best Friends works at the shelter-system level. These two programs are the reason POMDR exists as a separate organization and the reason CNN came. They should be named in the mission sentence (section 3.1 now carries the second) and they should be the second and third things on the homepage after the dogs, which today they are.

**On the Center for Nonprofit Excellence's resources.** The four principles are the useful part. The site's resource pages are a catalog of equity and governance reading, and its "Messaging Guidance for Nonprofits Under Pressure" is about answering political accusations, not about websites. Nothing further to take from it for this work.

## 11. Values, mission and vision at the front

POMDR already has the words. They are on the About page, folded under three closed questions a visitor has to open: "Our vision", "Our compassionate food policy", "Diversity, equity and inclusion". The live site's Culture page, which the old About linked to, has two words in the archive, so it was never written. What exists:

- **The vision, verbatim:** "Our vision is to model lifetime care for dogs and all companion animals, to help bring about a positive change in the way society thinks about and treats senior dogs, and to create better lives for them through rescue, foster, adoption, hospice and education."
- **The food policy, verbatim:** "We believe that all animals should be treated humanely and with compassion throughout their lives," and the vegetarian rule at every POMDR event.
- **DEI, verbatim:** "Diversity, equity and inclusion are important to POMDR. This includes staff, volunteers, adopters and Helping Paw clients."
- **The culture line, verbatim (About page today):** "kindness first, to dogs and to people."

Proposed: a short section on About called **"What we believe"**, open, not folded, before the team. Five lines, each a value already stated somewhere on the site, in POMDR's words:

1. **Lifetime care.** Every dog who comes to us is ours for life. *(the commitment, said everywhere)*
2. **Kindness first, to dogs and to people.** *(the culture line, already on About)*
3. **Compassion for every animal.** We serve only vegetarian food at every POMDR event. *(the food policy, made a sentence a visitor will actually read)*
4. **Everyone is welcome.** Adopters, fosters, volunteers and Helping Paw clients, without exception. *(DEI, in plain words, with the full policy still a tap away)*
5. **Honesty about what we can do.** We say what a dog needs and what we cannot take on. *(the limits the site already states, named as a value)*

The vision sentence stays verbatim under the five, as the long form. The three folds stay beneath for anyone who wants the policy text. This costs nothing POMDR has not already said, and it puts the values on the page instead of behind it. The Muttville comparison is the argument: a rescue with no values section is at the mercy of its tagline.

**A rule for every rewrite in this document:** before a sentence changes, ask which of the five values it serves. If it serves none, it is probably decoration. That is the test that keeps the voice POMDR's while the craft improves.
