# Levelling up the voice: what we add, what we change, what we remove, and why

**Proposal, written 2026-10-19 for Andrew Z.** Built from a full read of every word on the new site (`SITE-COPY-2026-10-19.md`, 41 pages, 17,100 words), the live site's archived voice, and two references Andrew asked me to study: the Center for Nonprofit Excellence's four principles of high-converting copy, and charity: water's site as the example of those principles done well. Nothing here is written yet. Every rewrite below is shown before and after so staff approve words, not ideas. Nothing invents a fact; where a number or a name is needed, it is marked as POMDR's to supply.

---

## 1. What the references teach, and what they do not

**The four principles (CNE)** are right and short: lead with the people and animals helped, not the process; put the mission above the fold; write at an eighth-grade level; make every button say what happens. Worth saying plainly: CNE's own homepage breaks three of its four rules. Its headline is "Celebrating 20 Years!", its buttons say "Learn More" five times, and it carries no impact number. The principles are the lesson, not the site.

**charity: water** is the site that keeps all four. What it does, in its own words:

- One promise as the headline: "Bring clean and safe water to every person on the planet." Not a tagline about the organisation; a thing the reader can join.
- Impact stated as counts a reader can picture: "209,241 water projects funded", "21,641,908 people will be served", each with a one-line definition of what the number means.
- Money translated into people: "A gift of just $20 a month is enough to bring six people, an entire family, clean water each year." And "$40 can end a walk for water."
- Proof as a section, not a claim: "We prove every project", "We're an open book", "You have our word, and more."
- Every button is an outcome: "Sponsor Now", "Give Today", "Browse Fundraisers". Never "Learn more" as the primary action.

**What we must not copy.** charity: water is a global organisation with a hundred million dollars behind it, and its voice is bright, fast and sure. POMDR is forty people in Pacific Grove taking the dogs nobody else will, talking to a widow about her terrier. Our register is quieter, slower and more tender, and that is the brand. The principles transfer. The pitch does not. "Bold" at POMDR means "We will not put your dog down for being old," which the intake page already says, and which is braver than anything on charity: water's homepage.

## 2. The POMDR voice, stated once

Five rules, drawn from the sentences on the site that already work. Everything in sections 3 to 5 serves these.

1. **The reader is in the sentence.** "Money should not be the reason you lose your dog." Not "Helping Paw provides financial assistance."
2. **A promise is said as a promise.** "We have a lifetime commitment to all the dogs who come into our care." Not "POMDR is dedicated to."
3. **Impact is a count of real dogs and real people, with what it cost.** "4,500 dogs" is a start. "Every dog gets a senior blood panel and, most of them, a dental" is the better sentence, because a reader can picture it.
4. **Honest about limits, in the same breath.** "Some extreme medical cases might be difficult for us to take on." Keep every one of these. They are what make the promises believable.
5. **Short words, short sentences, no process.** Eighth grade on the program pages, sixth on the care pages. No "processing of your request." No "subject to availability." If staff would not say it on the phone, it does not go on the page.

## 3. What we add

### 3.1 One mission sentence, everywhere the mission is stated

Today the mission is told six ways (homepage, Why, About, footer, Volunteer, Clinic). Proposed single sentence, in POMDR's words from the live site reordered for the reader:

> **We find homes for senior dogs, and we help senior people keep the dogs they love. Every dog who comes to us is ours for life.**

Used verbatim on the homepage above the fold, the About band, the footer, and the share description. Needs: Carie's yes.

### 3.2 A promise block, "You have our word"

charity: water's "You have our word, and more" section, in POMDR's register. Four promises the site already makes in four different places, gathered as one block for the About page and the donate page:

- We will not put a dog down for being old.
- Every dog gets a full exam, a senior blood panel and the dental work they need before they go home.
- If an adoption does not work out, for any reason, ever, the dog comes back to us.
- You can ask how your dog is doing, for the rest of their life.

Needs: nothing. Every line is on the site today.

### 3.3 Impact that a reader can picture

The site's only numbers are 4,500 dogs, 1,500 volunteers and 17 years, repeated on four pages. The 2024 Impact Report has more, and the clinic page has the most persuasive facts on the site, hidden under "Diagnostics on site." Proposed numbers band for the homepage, About and Donate, with the definition line charity: water uses:

### 3.4 A gift ladder with what each gift does

charity: water: "$20 a month brings six people clean water." POMDR's donate page has chips ($60, $125, $275, $600, $1,250) that say nothing. Proposed, once the clinic gives three real costs:

- **$60** "A month of medication for one dog in foster." *(cost to confirm)*
- **$125** "A senior blood panel, the first thing we do for every new dog."
- **$275** "A dental cleaning. Most of our dogs need one."
- **$600** "Extractions and the dental under anaesthetic."
- **$1,250** "A dog's whole first month: exam, bloods, dental, chip, vaccines, and a foster home."

Needs: the five costs from Kelly or Monica. The copy is ready to drop in.

### 3.6 A dog-story standard, for the admin

Dog stories are the most-read words on the site and the only ones without a standard. Adele's story (quiet, specific, foster's own observation) is the model. Proposed prompt for the intake form, which goes to Ronnie as part of the harmony proposal:

> "Two or three short paragraphs a stranger reads first. Open with who this dog is at home. Say what they love and what they need, in the foster's words. Skip breed, age and weight; the page shows those. End with a sentence, not a question."

And three rules for the page: no "Meet [Name]" opener, no question ending, no puns longer than one line.

### 3.7 The named person

charity: water shows "The executive team." POMDR's care pages should name who answers: "You will reach Shanel or Alejandra, Monday to Saturday." A name is the strongest trust signal a small rescue has. Needs: POMDR-47 items 6 and 7.

## 4. What we change

### 4.1 Headlines that put the reader in the sentence

| Today | Proposed |
|---|---|
| Our staff is family. (Jobs) | Work where the dogs are the point. |
| Our team is awesome. (About) | The people who answer the phone. |
| Senior dogs and senior people, helped together. (numbers) | What your gifts did last year. |
| Programs for senior dogs and senior people. (homepage) | Three ways we keep dogs and people together. |
| Care for the dogs in our rescue, and beyond. (Clinic) | A clinic of our own, so no dog is turned away. |
| Hear from our clients. (homepage) | In their words. |

### 4.2 The carried-over paragraphs, rewritten in the care voice

These sit beside warm new copy and the seam shows. Before and after:

**Adoption process, step one.** Today: "Our dogs are kept in loving foster homes, so the first step to meeting an adoptable dog is the online questionnaire. Responses are typically sent by email; please check your spam or junk folder for the latest updates on your adoption." Proposed: "Every dog lives in a foster home, so the first step is the questionnaire. We reply by email within a few days. If you do not hear from us, check your spam folder, or call."

**Surrender, step one.** Today: "Fill out the online intake questionnaire. If you need help with it, call us at (831) 718-9122; please be aware that this will delay the processing of your request." Proposed: "Tell us about your dog in the questionnaire, or call (831) 718-9122 and we will fill it in with you."

**Perpetual Care, step one.** Today: "Contact POMDR and talk with a board member to determine whether your dog is appropriate for our program. Eligibility depends on the dog's age, size, temperament and any special medical needs, and we will need to meet your dog." Proposed: "Call us. A board member will talk with you about your dog, and we will come and meet them. Age, size, temperament and health all matter, and none of them rules a dog out on its own."

**Helping Paw, volunteer help.** Today: "Short term dog walking for emergency situations; emergency short term foster care or boarding for people who are in hospital or recovering from illness or surgery; transportation to the vet or groomer for disabled and senior pet guardians." Proposed: "A volunteer walks your dog when you cannot. If you go into hospital, we foster or board your dog until you are home. If you cannot drive, we take them to the vet or the groomer."

**Donate, monthly.** Today: "Become a sustaining donor and pledge a recurring monthly donation. Your credit card will be charged your pledged amount at the first of every month." Proposed: "Give the same amount on the first of every month. It is what lets us say yes to the next dog before the money is in. Change or stop it any time."

### 4.3 Helping Paw described once

Four pages list what Helping Paw covers, and the lists differ. One list, from the program page, used everywhere it is mentioned: **walking, rides to the vet, help with a vet bill, food and supplies, and a foster home while you are in hospital.** The homepage, Why, About and Volunteer pages take that sentence verbatim.

### 4.5 Repetition made deliberate

"You can stop part way through and come back to it" appears on seven form pages and "Any trouble at all, call" on five. Keep both; they are reassurance at the anxious moment. Remove the rest: the four mission variants (3.1), the four Helping Paw lists (4.3), the "No dog is ever left behind" paragraph that appears on Hospice and Why word for word (keep on Hospice; Why now has the promise strip).

## 5. What we remove, and why

- **Every "Learn more" and bare "Learn More" as a primary action.** There are few, which is good. Replace the ones that remain with the outcome (3.5).
- **Process language a visitor does not need:** "processing of your request", "subject to volunteer availability in the area" (say "where we have a volunteer near you"), "evaluated case by case" (say "we will talk about it"), "consistent with the information in their Pet Profile".
- **Question endings on dog stories** ("Could that be with you?") and "Meet [Name]" openers. Staff-written, so by standard (3.6), not by edit.
- **"Our team is awesome."** Replace with a sentence that shows it: "Twenty-seven people, most with a POMDR dog of their own."
- **The second "No dog is ever left behind" block** on Why, now that the promise strip carries the same promise.
- **"It is a promise, not a program"** on the homepage's Perpetual Care card. A nice line that undercuts the word "program" used in the page title two taps away.
- **Four small errors:** "ExclusiveYou must apply" on Resources, the stray bracket in "(I've Got a) Golden Ticket)", "Alberston" for Albertson, and the sponsor page's "You are sponsoring . Thank you." when no dog is named (hide the line until a name is present).

## 6. What does not change

- Every verbatim quote, including grammar and "fur baby." They are the proof.
- Every promise already made, and every honest limit stated.
- The care-page voice: "You are doing the right thing by asking." "We will not rush you." These are the standard the rest is being raised to.
- Phone first on every care page. charity: water never shows a phone number. POMDR always does, and should.
- The two colours, the band, the button above the text.
- Spanish on the Helping Paw and Surrender paths.

## 8. Order of work, and how we check it

1. **Mission sentence and promise block** (3.1, 3.2): half a day once Carie answers. Changes six pages.
2. **Carried-over paragraphs and headlines** (4.1, 4.2): one day. Shown before and after in one document for staff.
3. **Helping Paw reconciled, repetition cut, small errors** (4.3, 4.5, 5): half a day.
4. **Microcopy** (3.5): half a day, then the paper button test from POMDR-25: read the button, say what happens.
5. **Impact band and gift ladder** (3.3, 3.4): the hour the numbers arrive.
6. **Story standard** (3.6): to Ronnie with the harmony proposal.

Checks on every change: reading grade per page against the table in 4.4, the voice rules (no em dashes, no question endings, no "Meet"), the stress harness on journeys 2, 4 and 6, and the full page render. Then the test with people, because wording is the thing a machine cannot judge.
