# The storytelling sweep: every section page, read as a visitor

**Written 2026-10-16 for Andrew Z.** The third document of the day, after the thesis (what the pages measure) and the council (what visitors manage to do). This one is about what each page *says*, in what order, with what pictures, and whether it reads like a page a hundred careful people have looked at. Every section page was rendered at 1280px, scrolled end to end, and read against the live site's archived words (`docs/archive/live-site-2026-10-16/`), so nothing the old site said is lost by accident.

## 1. Why this week's work matters, in one paragraph

Three weeks ago the site was a plain-PHP rebuild with good bones and bare pages. Since then it has gained a menu organised by what a visitor wants (not by POMDR's org chart), an opening band on every page that names the page's one job and puts the action above the words, a kit of nine storytelling patterns that every body is built from, team pages with the real roster and a story card, a homepage that reads properly on a phone, dog pages that end in the ask instead of a gap, and two rounds of measurement (71 pages by script, a thousand simulated visits, three independent reviewers) whose findings are nearly all fixed. The result is a site that an older reader on a phone can use with large text and no JavaScript, that loads one photograph on the first screen instead of four, and that says the same thing in the menu, the band and the button. What this sweep adds is the last layer: reading each page as a story.

## 2. The archive of the live site

`docs/archive/live-site-2026-10-16/` holds every page reachable from the live homepage (87 section pages, 27 person pages, three sample dog pages of the 80 seen): one Markdown file per page with the words in page order, every image with its address and alt text, every form, frame and standalone link, the navigation as presented, and the raw HTML. `README.md` there is the index with word counts. Three things in it the new site does not yet have, and should (section 4): the **Thank You** donor page (1,151 words of named donors), **Max's Helping Paws Fund** (276 words, a named fund with its own giving form), and the **Culture** page (which on the live site is two words and a picture, so the content is still owed). The Lifetime Care FAQ, the plaque and dog-tag sponsorship pages, Wish List, Silver Hearts, Legacy and Planned Giving are all folded into the new Donate and Perpetual Care pages, and their words are in the archive if a sentence is ever wanted back.

## 3. Page by page

Each page is scored on: **arc** (hook, what we do, how it works, proof, ask), **type** (measure, hierarchy, no orphan words), **space** (the 64/32/12 rhythm, nothing crowded, nothing stranded), **pictures** (real, placed where they help), and **ask** (above the text and again at the end). A tick means it holds; a line means what was changed today or what remains.

## 4. What is still missing, from the archive

In the order a visitor would miss it:

1. **Thank You** (donor recognition, 1,151 words on the live site, plus the plaque and stone sponsor lists at 498 words). People look for their own name. A page under Give, built as a directory by year, is a morning's work once POMDR confirms the lists are current.
2. **News / What's Happening** (13 items on the live site). Fills the Events page and the "Recently" strip the council asked for. Needs POMDR to say which items are still worth showing.
3. **Max's Helping Paws Fund** (276 words, its own giving link). A card under "Give for a dog" on Donate with the fund's own paragraph; the words are in the archive.
4. **Culture**: the live page is a title and a picture; the words were never written. POMDR-11.
5. **Privacy and Terms**: the live text exists (3,778 words) and can be moved over as is, with a date, if POMDR approves it.

## 5. The standard this sweep held to

A page earns a tick when a visitor who reads only the band knows what the page is for and can act; when a visitor who reads everything meets the hook, the explanation, the proof and the ask in that order; when every heading is a sentence a person would say, not a label; when nothing repeats what the band already said; when a quote looks like every other quote; when the one deep band is the close; and when the type sits at its measure with no stranded word or card. Twenty-six pages were read against that. Seven changed today; the rest already held, which is the point: the kit is doing its work, and what remains is words only POMDR can supply.
