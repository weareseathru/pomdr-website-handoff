# POMDR photo screening guide

Status: DRAFT 2026-11-12, waiting for Andrew's approval. Built from Andrew's
brief and from the three photos he removed from "website photos 2.0" (a group
in sunglasses under hard sun, a dog held up and looking uneasy, an event with
empty chairs in front).

The goal: every photo on the site shows the joy of senior dogs and the people
who love them, with room for the tenderness too. A photo has to pass every
gate in part 1, then earn its place in part 2.

## 1. Gates: any one of these and the photo is out

1. **A dog that looks distressed or uneasy.** Wide eyes showing the whites,
   ears pinned back, tail tucked, pulling away, hard panting, being held up in
   the air or at arm's length, or squirming in someone's grip.
2. **A person who does not look happy or at ease.** Eyes closed, mid-word,
   squinting, a stiff or forced smile, looking at a phone.
3. **Sunglasses on the main person.** Eyes carry the feeling. Allowed only in
   a wide group or event shot where no single face is the subject.
4. **An awkward angle.** Shot from far above or below, heads or paws cut off,
   the subject small or at the edge of the frame, a tilted horizon, backs of
   heads.
5. **An event that looks empty.** Empty chairs or tables in the foreground,
   a thin crowd, people standing apart and not talking.
6. **Poor light.** Hard midday shadows across faces, blown-out sky or white
   fur, the subject in silhouette, flash glare, dim or grainy indoor shots.
7. **A distracting setting.** Holiday or studio backdrops, clutter, trash
   bins, cars, kennels or cages behind the subject, drinks front and center,
   another organization's branding.
8. **A weak file.** Under 2,400 px on the long edge, eyes not in focus,
   heavy filters, screenshots, text or stickers on the image.
9. **Out of date.** Older than three years, unless it is a historical photo
   used as one.

## 2. What earns a place

Each photo that passes the gates is scored out of 10. Seven or more goes in;
five or six goes to a "maybe" pile that Andrew sees; under five is out. Ties
go to the newer photo.

| What | Points | What I look for |
|---|---|---|
| Feeling | 0 to 3 | Real smiles or laughter; a person and dog looking at each other or leaning in; a relaxed dog with soft eyes and a loose mouth; touch, such as a hand on a gray muzzle or a cheek on a dog's head |
| Light | 0 to 2 | Soft daylight or open shade, faces and fur evenly lit, a catchlight in the eyes |
| Framing | 0 to 2 | Taken at the subject's eye level, the subject clear and large, a clean background such as the teal wall or the garden |
| POMDR in the picture | 0 to 1 | A Peace of Mind shirt, the logo, the sign, or an Adopt Me bandana. A plus, never required |
| The story | 0 to 2 | Fits a page's job: senior people with senior dogs, fosters, volunteers at work, staff giving care, a crowd enjoying an event |

By kind of photo:

- **A person with a dog.** Both look content. The best show a bond: eye
  contact between them, a laugh, a dog settled in a lap. Senior people with
  senior dogs are the heart of the story.
- **A dog alone.** Eye level, eyes sharp, good light, a cute or characterful
  expression. Gray muzzles are a plus, since that is who POMDR is for.
- **Events.** People talking, laughing or enjoying the dogs. The frame is
  full of people, at least one dog is in view, and POMDR's sign or shirts
  place it.
- **Groups.** Everyone visible, most faces to the camera, in good light.
- **The clinic.** Hands on a calm dog, staff faces visible, a tidy room. No
  wounds, blood or shaved surgical sites.

## 3. The balance across the site

- **Joy first, with depth.** About two photos in three show happiness and
  play. About one in three shows the quiet side: a still moment, an old dog
  resting against someone, a hand on a gray face. Tender, never sad or
  pitiful.
- **Fit for the spot.** The home slideshow needs wide photos with calm space
  on the left for the words. Small tiles need room around the subject so a
  square crop keeps the faces.
- **Recent and varied.** Newest first; no two photos of the same person and
  dog on one page; a mix of people, dog sizes and settings.
- **Only Andrew and Carie can judge** whether a person has agreed to be shown
  (Helping Paw guardians above all) and whether a dog is still with POMDR.

## How it gets better

Every photo Andrew removes is logged with his reason, and the guide is
updated so the same mistake is not made twice.

2. Holiday and studio backdrops: out of the website set entirely, or kept

3. Medical realism: a cone or a bandage on the clinic and hospice pages, yes
