Peace of Mind Dog Rescue · new website

Website Photo Pass

Every photo on the new site, where it sits, whether it is sharp enough for the spot, and a box to tick for each one. Prepared 9 November 2026 for Andrew Z. and Carie.

How to use this packet

  1. Go page by page. Each photo has a number, a thumbnail, the words it sits under, and a rating. Tick Keep, Replace (write the photo you want in its place), or Take a new photo.
  2. Start with the red and amber ratings. Red means the file is smaller than the space it fills, so it looks blurry everywhere. Amber means it looks fine on an older screen but soft on a phone or a new laptop. Green is sharp.
  3. For dogs, circle the best main photo. Each dog has a numbered strip of every photo on their page. Write the number of the photo that should lead, or tick the box to ask the foster for new ones.
  4. Hand the packet back to Andrew. The last page explains how the chosen photos get onto the site.

20photos already prepared and sitting unused, at the back of the packet. Some may fill a Replace box straight away.

What makes the right photo for a spot

The picture

  • A real POMDR dog or person, never stock.
  • Eyes in focus, natural light, the dog's face clear of leashes and hands.
  • Matches the job of the page: a calm senior dog for adoption, a guardian with their dog for Helping Paw, hands giving care for the clinic.
  • The dog is still in the program, or the page is about a happy tail.

The file

  • The shape matches the spot: wide spots need wide photos, square tiles need room around the subject.
  • Full-size originals only. A photo saved from Facebook, a text message or a screenshot is already shrunk and cannot be made sharp again.
  • Wide banners: 2,000 px wide or more. Photos in a column: 1,200 px. Small tiles: 800 px. Any phone camera original is 3,000 px or more, which covers all of them.
  • Everyone pictured has said yes, especially Helping Paw clients.

After the meeting: how the photos get onto the site

  1. One shared folder. Put every chosen photo in one Google Drive folder, full size, and name each file with its number from this packet, for example 1.3 home slideshow mission.jpg. Uploading to Drive from a phone keeps the original. Texting or emailing a photo usually shrinks it, so avoid both.
  2. Page photos (Part 1 and Part 4). Andrew prepares each one with the site's photo tool, which sizes it for its spot, makes the phone versions, and strips the hidden location data cameras add. The new photo keeps the old file's name, so no page has to change. One batch per group of pages, checked on the preview before it goes live.
  3. Team portraits (Part 2). Upload on the person's record in the admin. Square, face centered, plain background, the same style for everyone.
  4. Dogs (Part 3). Upload new photos to the dog in Shelterluv, full size from the phone. The site copies them in as they arrive. Then use Make main photo on the dog's record in the site admin for the one circled.
  5. The check. Andrew re-runs the same measurement that made this packet and confirms every red and amber spot is now green, before the site goes live.
