## Our Adoption Process

*Address:* `/process/`  
*Browser title:* Our Adoption Process | Peace of Mind Dog Rescue  
*Share description:* What we do for every dog before adoption, and the steps to meeting one.

## How adopting works
Three steps, and we help you through each one.
Most adoptions take about a week. You fill in a questionnaire, we talk, you meet the dog, and the dog goes home with you.
[button] Start an adoption questionnaire

### Three steps to a new best friend.

#### Fill out the adoption questionnaire
Our dogs are kept in loving foster homes, so the first step to meeting an adoptable dog is the online questionnaire. Responses are typically sent by email; please check your spam or junk folder for the latest updates on your adoption.
Start the questionnaire

#### Talk with the foster, then meet the dog
If you are a good match, we will connect you with the dog's foster to talk more, then set up a meet and greet with the dog.

#### Home check, then welcome home
All adopters agree to an initial home check and annual follow-up calls throughout the life of the dog. We take our role of placing dogs in a new home very seriously, and do everything we can to ensure the dogs we place are well cared for throughout their lives.

### Every dog arrives ready.
Each dog in our program receives a thorough veterinary exam upon intake. The senior dogs also get a senior blood panel and, in many cases, a dental cleaning and any necessary extractions. Each dog is altered, microchipped and vaccinated, unless our veterinarian advises against sterilization surgery or vaccines due to age or other health concerns.
Adoption fees range from $155 to $305, depending on the age and health of the dog and the expected future medical expenses that will be taken on by the adopter.

## Why a Senior Dog

## Why a senior dog
The best dogs are sometimes the oldest.
Peace of Mind Dog Rescue exists for the dogs other shelters overlook: the gray-muzzled, the slow-walking, the deeply loyal. Here is why a senior dog may be the right dog, and why supporting them matters.
[button] Meet the dogs
[button] Foster a senior dog
[image alt] A gray-muzzled senior dog resting at home.

### Senior dogs and senior people, together.
Founded in 2009 on California's Central Coast, POMDR helps senior dogs find loving homes and helps senior people keep the dogs they love. Older dogs are at the highest risk in shelters and the least likely to be chosen. We changed that math for our community, one dog at a time.

#### They are already who they are
A senior dog's size, temperament and energy are settled. What you see is who you get: no guessing, no surprises as they grow.

#### Calm company, gentle pace
Many older dogs are house-trained, leash-mannered, and happy with a short walk and a long nap. They fit a calmer life beautifully.

#### Gratitude you can feel
Dogs who have waited the longest tend to bond the deepest. A second chance late in life is not lost on them.

#### If it ever does not work out, they come back to us
We have a lifetime commitment to all the dogs who come into our care. An adoption that does not work out is not held against anyone. The dog comes back to POMDR, and we find the right next home.

### Honest about the health, and the cost.
Each dog in our program receives a thorough veterinary exam upon intake. The senior dogs also get a senior blood panel and, in many cases, a dental cleaning and any necessary extractions. We share each dog's health honestly, so you choose with open eyes.
Adoption fees range from $155 to $305, depending on the age and health of the dog and the expected future medical expenses that will be taken on by the adopter. We never leave an adopter to navigate care alone.
How adopting works
[image alt] A small black dog with a gray muzzle wearing a blue Adopt Me bandana, in front of a mural.

### Caring for the people too.
Senior dogs are often surrendered for heartbreaking reasons. An owner enters care, a health crisis hits, or the bills become impossible. The dog did nothing wrong, and neither did the person.
Our Helping Paw program supports senior people and people with disabilities so they can keep their dogs at home. Walking help, financial assistance for veterinary care, and temporary foster during a hospital stay all keep families together.
When staying together is not possible, we welcome the dog into our care and find the right next home. No one is judged. Everyone is helped.
About Helping Paw

### Waiting for a couch.
We typically have about 80 dogs in our care at any given time, and up to 15 dogs waiting to get into foster homes. POMDR provides the crate, bed, collar, harness, leash, ID tag, bowls, toys and flea prevention, and covers all medical expenses. We ask that our foster homes provide the food and a loving, safe home until your foster dog is adopted.

## Perpetual Care Program

## Perpetual Care
What happens to your dog if something happens to you.
Enroll your dog now and POMDR promises to take them in and find them a home if you die or can no longer care for them. It is the worry that keeps a lot of older owners awake.
[button] Call (831) 718-9122 about Perpetual Care
[image alt] A woman kissing the head of a scruffy tan terrier in front of a painted mural.

### A promise made now, for a day you hope never comes.
If there is nobody in your life who could take your dog, enroll them with us. The day you cannot care for them, we will. Your dog comes into a POMDR foster home, with the routines and the vet records you told us about, and stays there for the rest of their life or until we find them a new person as carefully as we would for any dog of ours.
It costs nothing during your lifetime. What it buys is the thing none of us likes to think about and all of us want settled: that your dog will never wake up alone in a shelter wondering where you went.
[button] Call (831) 718-9122 about Perpetual Care
[button] Listen to the NPR story
[image alt] A man in a blue shirt holding two small gray-muzzled dogs against his cheeks.

### How to enroll.

#### Talk with us
Contact POMDR and talk with a board member to determine whether your dog is appropriate for our program. Eligibility depends on the dog's age, size, temperament and any special medical needs, and we will need to meet your dog.

#### Fill out a Pet Profile
So we know your dog's likes, dislikes, habits, routines and health history, which vet clinic has their records, and anything else you would like us to know.

#### Set up a pet trust with your attorney
A legal pet trust for the lifetime care expenses of your dog in the event of your illness, injury or death. There is no membership fee or cost to you during your lifetime. If you are unable to set up a pet trust but would still like to enroll your dog, please contact us to discuss the next steps.

#### Consider an annual gift
We also suggest an annual gift, in any amount, to POMDR to support our mission to be here for dogs left behind. At the time of your dog's death, any remainder of the trust goes into POMDR's general fund to help more dogs.

### Why plan now.
Do you have written instructions with the name and contact details of the person who will care for your dog? Has the new guardian agreed, in no uncertain terms, to make your dog a part of their family if you can no longer care for them?
None of us likes to imagine what our life would be like without our dog, or worse, what our dog's life would be like without us. Once these arrangements are made, you can have peace of mind that your dog will not end up alone and frightened in an animal shelter.
Enrolled dogs receive placement in a loving foster home at any point that you are no longer able to care for them, and a lifetime commitment from POMDR: your dog is cared for by a POMDR foster home or a new adoptive home for the rest of their life.
[image alt] A hand resting on the head of a white bull terrier mix with a pink nose.

### Questions people ask us.
The two questions we hear most are open already. This page prints well, if you would like a copy for your papers.
What if my dog is old or in poor health?
If you enroll your dog in the Perpetual Care Program, POMDR will accept your dog regardless of their physical condition. Although older and ailing animals are sometimes difficult to place in permanent homes, POMDR will provide foster care for your dog as long as needed. You will want to take your dog's age and health into account when funding the pet trust.
What will the program provide if I die or can no longer care for my dog?
POMDR will become your dog's legal guardian, and will start caring for your pet as soon as we receive notice of your death or incapacitation. We will then place your pet up for adoption to a suitable, loving home, consistent with the information in their Pet Profile. Enrollment ensures that your dog will receive compassionate care for the rest of their life.

### One of the most loving decisions you can make.
Some of the dogs we have loved most came to us this way: from a person whose health or life had changed, and who loved their dog enough to ask for help. If that is where you are, we will help you find the next home. We work with people and dogs in Monterey, Santa Cruz and San Benito counties.

### How it works.

#### Tell us about your dog
Fill out the online intake questionnaire. If you need help with it, call us at (831) 718-9122; please be aware that this will delay the processing of your request.

#### We meet your dog
Before POMDR can take in your dog, we will need to meet the dog. Dogs who come to POMDR must be good with other dogs (no aggression) and must be good with people (no bite history or propensity to bite). Medical intakes are evaluated case by case.

#### Your dog moves to a foster home
Placement in a foster home until adoption, with high quality food, coverage of any medical needs that come up, grooming and flea control, socialization and companionship.

#### We find the right home, and stay in touch
Screening of potential adopters, including a home visit and reference check, then annual follow-ups and home visits to ensure the placement has been successful. A safety net for the lifetime of your dog, should they ever need a new home again.

Advocating for seniors. Dogs and people alike.

### Senior dogs, senior people, and the promise between them.
We find loving foster and forever homes for dogs whose guardians can no longer care for them, and for senior dogs in shelters. We also provide volunteer and financial assistance so seniors can keep their pets with them as long as possible. In addition, we make pre-arrangements to take in dogs should their guardians become unable to care for them.
We have a lifetime commitment to all the dogs who come into our care. They will either be adopted into a wonderful, permanent home, or will live out their lives in one of our foster homes.

## Veterinary clinic
Quality care, kept affordable.
The Harry and Jaynne Boand Veterinary Clinic in Monterey provides accessible, compassionate veterinary care for our rescue dogs and for the senior people and dogs we serve across California's Central Coast.
[button] Call (831) 718-9122
[button] What we offer
[image alt] The Harry and Jaynne Boand Veterinary Clinic in Monterey.

### Visit us
1251 10th Street Monterey, CA 93940
By appointment. Please call first.

### Call us
To make an appointment or ask a question, call (831) 718-9122.

### Email us
Reach the team at info@pomdr.org.

### Care for the dogs in our rescue, and beyond.
The clinic opened in November 2019 and is the medical home for every dog in our care. It is where new arrivals get a clean start and where our long-term and hospice dogs are kept comfortable. The same clinic helps us deliver the Helping Paw program for guardians who need a hand with veterinary costs.

#### Intake and wellness exams
Every dog who comes into our rescue starts with a full health check, so we understand their needs and can match them with the right home.

#### Vaccines and preventive care
Routine vaccinations, parasite prevention, and the ongoing care that keeps our dogs healthy while they wait for adoption.

#### Diagnostics on site
Every new dog receives a full exam, senior blood panel, heartworm test, urinalysis, vaccines and a microchip. We have a full x-ray machine, a dental x-ray machine and ultrasound, so we can look closer when something needs attention.

#### Dental care, surgery and lumps
Senior dogs often arrive with neglected teeth and other conditions. Most need a dental cleaning and many need extractions. We also aspirate, biopsy or remove any lumps that need it, treating conditions other shelters often cannot take on.

#### Hospice and comfort care
For our hospice and sanctuary dogs, the clinic focuses on comfort, pain management and dignity through every stage of life.

#### Specialist referrals
When a dog needs more, we refer to veterinary specialists including ophthalmology, oncology, dermatology, dentistry and internal medicine.

### Good medicine is what makes a second chance possible.
Many of the dogs who come to us are seniors. Some have been overlooked for years, and some arrive with medical needs that other shelters could not take on. A clinic of our own means we never have to turn a dog away because care felt out of reach.
Founded in 2009, Peace of Mind Dog Rescue serves senior dogs and senior people across the Central Coast. The clinic is a cornerstone of that promise, keeping costs manageable so more dogs can heal and more families can stay together.

Volunteer applications doubled, adoption applications tripled, Helping Paw applications quadrupled and we rose to the challenge. We are grateful to be able to continue to serve those in our community who are most vulnerable. This video features the song "Rise Up" as sung by Andra Day.
Created by Monica Rua, Board President/Co-Founder, 2020

## Monica Rua
Co-founder/Non Profit Consultant
Advisory Council
[image alt] Monica Rua
Monica currently shares her home with four rescue dogs from POMDR: Zeta, Zorro, Katmai and Frolic. She is usually fostering one or two POMDR dogs also.
Monica has devoted over 27 years of her life to animal rescue. She first worked with dog and cat rescue groups in the Bay Area. In 1998 she co-founded AFRP in Pacific Grove, where she served on the board for 11 years, with 8 of those years as Board President. Monica oversaw and actively participated in all aspects of the organization’s operations, including educating the public, fundraising, volunteer database management, animal tracking, and fostering many dogs and cats. She was also instrumental in helping foster families with dog behavior issues.
Monica was on the POMDR board from 2009, when she co-founded this organization with Carie Broecker, until June 2024 when she moved to the advisory council.
Monica has been studying animal behavior and positive reinforcement techniques for 20+ years. She brings her knowledge to POMDR to help with behavior assessments and troubleshooting dog behavior issues with staff, fosters, and adopters. Monica also helps with photography, graphic design, web design, communications, and strategic planning.
Before launching into animal rescue, Monica was the Education Director of Happy Hollow Zoo in San Jose. Prior to that, she worked as a software engineer for NASA Johnson Space Center, Lockheed, and Sun Microsystems.
Monica holds a Master of Science in Computer Science: Artificial Intelligence (MSAI) from Stanford University and a Bachelor of Science in Computer Science from Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute with a minor in Philosophy.
